# Project Chronos — Scientific Principles

**Scientific Integrity, Verification, and Responsible Research**

## 1. Purpose

Project Chronos aims to advance scientific knowledge through artificial intelligence, computational research, theoretical investigation, and international collaboration.

Its research interests include autonomous intelligence, information preservation, distributed computing, spacetime, time, and causality.

Because some of these subjects involve highly speculative questions, the project requires a rigorous scientific framework.

This document establishes the principles governing research, experimentation, evaluation, and publication within Project Chronos.

## 2. Evidence Over Expectations

Scientific conclusions must be determined by evidence rather than by the expectations, ambitions, or beliefs of researchers.

No hypothesis should receive preferential treatment simply because it supports the project's long-term vision.

Chronos must remain capable of rejecting its own assumptions.

## 3. Classification of Scientific Claims

Research findings should be classified according to their level of scientific support.

### Level 1 — Established Experimental Knowledge

Findings supported by reliable experimental evidence and independent scientific verification.

### Level 2 — Established Theoretical Frameworks

Mathematical theories with substantial scientific support, even where particular predictions or applications remain experimentally untested.

### Level 3 — Theoretical Proposals

Mathematical models, proposed mechanisms, and theoretical interpretations that have not yet received sufficient experimental confirmation.

### Level 4 — Exploratory Hypotheses

New ideas generated through human reasoning, computational methods, or artificial intelligence that require further examination.

### Level 5 — Speculation

Ideas for which a consistent mathematical formulation, supporting evidence, or a testable prediction has not yet been established.

These classifications describe the current evidence for a claim, not its permanent scientific status. New evidence may change its classification.

## 4. Independent Verification

AI-generated results must never be accepted as scientific discoveries solely because an AI system produced them.

Important results should undergo appropriate verification, including:

- Mathematical derivation and consistency checks.
- Comparison with established scientific literature.
- Independent computational reproduction.
- Evaluation by qualified researchers.
- Experimental testing where possible.

The verification method should be appropriate to the claim being examined.

## 5. Reproducibility and Transparency

Research should be documented in sufficient detail to allow independent examination.

Whenever appropriate, this includes:

- Source code and software versions.
- Hardware configurations.
- Experimental procedures.
- Input data and relevant datasets.
- Mathematical assumptions.
- Processing methods.
- Results, uncertainties, and limitations.

Changes to research methods should be recorded so that earlier results can be understood and reproduced.

## 6. Negative Results and Failed Hypotheses

Failure is an essential part of scientific research.

Project Chronos will document meaningful negative results, unsuccessful experiments, and hypotheses that fail verification.

Researchers must not hide contradictory evidence or modify experimental criteria after observing results merely to obtain a desired conclusion.

A well-documented negative result can prevent other researchers from repeating unsuccessful approaches.

## 7. Research Into Time and Causality

The investigation of temporal information transfer requires particular care.

Project Chronos recognizes that:

- Time dilation is an experimentally established consequence of relativity.
- Wormholes and closed timelike curves appear in certain theoretical models, but their physical realizability is not established.
- Mathematical solutions do not automatically demonstrate that a corresponding physical system can exist.
- No experimentally verified method for transmitting new information into the past is currently known.

Any proposed future-to-past information transfer mechanism must be examined for consistency with established physical laws.

A credible experiment must exclude ordinary communication, prior information, predictive inference, statistical coincidences, and experimental artifacts.

## 8. Research Independence

Scientific conclusions must remain independent of financial, institutional, political, and personal interests.

Contributors should disclose relevant conflicts of interest.

Funding and institutional partnerships may support research but must not dictate its conclusions.

## 9. Responsible Artificial Intelligence

Autonomous research systems should operate within clearly defined permissions and safety boundaries.

Project Chronos will prioritize:

- Human oversight for consequential actions.
- Transparent research records.
- Secure handling of data and credentials.
- Controlled access to computing resources.
- Protection against unauthorized system modification.
- Evaluation of AI-generated research before publication.

Autonomy should improve research efficiency without eliminating scientific accountability.

## 10. Open Scientific Collaboration

Project Chronos encourages constructive criticism and independent contributions.

Research proposals, software, experiments, and conclusions should be open to examination.

Disagreement is not a threat to the project. It is an essential part of scientific progress.

## 11. Continuous Revision

Scientific principles and research procedures may evolve as the project develops.

Changes must be documented and should strengthen scientific integrity, reproducibility, transparency, and responsible research.

## Final Principle

**Project Chronos must never value a desired discovery more than the truth.**

Its purpose is not to prove a predetermined hypothesis, but to investigate difficult questions honestly and contribute whatever knowledge the evidence supports.
